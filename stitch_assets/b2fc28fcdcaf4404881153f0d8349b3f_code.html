lightbulb Key Takeaway

APA 7th Edition requires capitalizing all words of four letters or more, including conjunctions and prepositions. Only minor words like 'and', 'but', 'for', and 'or' under four letters remain lowercase unless they are the first word.

General Capitalization Rules

Navigating the complexities of APA 7th edition doesn't have to be a daunting task. The primary shift from the previous edition involves how we handle longer prepositions and conjunctions. In APA 7, the length of the word is the primary driver for capitalization.

Capitalize These Words

  • The first word of the title or heading
  • The first word after a colon, em dash, or end punctuation in a heading
  • Both words in a hyphenated compound
  • All words of four letters or more

Pro-Tip: Managing Hyphens

APA 7 changed the rule for hyphenated words. Both words in a hyphenated compound should now be capitalized (e.g., "Peer-to-Peer" instead of "Peer-to-peer").

Exceptions to the Rule

While the "four-letter rule" covers most bases, there are still minor words (articles, short prepositions, and coordinating conjunctions) that remain lowercase unless they appear at the start of a title.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is "with" capitalized in APA 7th edition? expand_more
Yes, "With" is capitalized in APA 7th Edition titles because it contains four letters. APA rules state that all words with four or more letters must be capitalized.
How do you handle words after a colon? expand_more
The first word after a colon in a title or heading is always capitalized, regardless of its length or part of speech.
Do you capitalize "a", "an", and "the"? expand_more
These articles are only capitalized if they are the first word of the title or follow a colon/em-dash. Otherwise, they remain lowercase.
What about hyphenated words in titles? expand_more
In APA 7th Edition, both parts of a hyphenated word should be capitalized in a title or heading (e.g., "Long-Term Study").
